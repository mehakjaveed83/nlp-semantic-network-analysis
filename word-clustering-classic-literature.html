Full Pipeline

From raw text to refined graph

Stage 1
Preprocess
lemmatise · clean

Raw .txt files for all three novels are stripped of Project Gutenberg headers and footers, lowercased, and stripped of punctuation. Sentence tokenisation (NLTK) precedes stop-word removal and lemmatisation, yielding the top 100 content words — verbs, nouns, adjectives, and proper nouns — across roughly 450k combined tokens.

Stage 2
Build Metric
co-occurrence · distance

For every word pair, shared sentences are counted and the mean token gap across the corpus is computed. These combine into a hybrid distance: frequent co-occurrence shrinks the distance, while large positional separation increases it.

Stage 3
Dijkstra Refine
graph smoothing

The 100 × 100 hybrid distance matrix becomes a weighted graph — words as nodes, distances as edge weights. Dijkstra's shortest-path algorithm then captures transitive similarity: words that rarely co-occur directly can still be close if connected through shared intermediaries.

Stage 4
Cluster + Visualise
KMeans · DBSCAN · …

Four unsupervised methods — K-Means, Hierarchical, Spectral, and DBSCAN — run on both the raw and Dijkstra-refined matrices. PCA projects the result to two dimensions for direct visual comparison.

Custom Distance Metric

A metric built from corpus statistics alone

Hybrid distance, word i · word j
Dij  =  1 / (1 + co-occij)  +  avg_positionalij
co-occij sentences where i and j both appear
avg_positional mean token-index gap between i and j

No pre-trained embeddings

The inverse co-occurrence term pulls frequently paired words closer together, while the positional term penalises pairs that, even when they do co-occur, tend to sit far apart within a sentence.

Every value in the matrix is derived directly from the three-novel corpus — there is no external model, no transfer learning, and no pre-trained vector space underneath it.

Sentence co-occurrence → count shared sentences per pair
Average positional distance → mean token gap across corpus
Dijkstra Shortest-Path Refinement

Transitive similarity, not just direct contact

The raw hybrid matrix only sees pairs that co-occur directly. Treating the matrix as a weighted graph and running Dijkstra's algorithm over it surfaces a shortest-path matrix that also captures indirect, transitive relationships.

A word pair connected only through shared intermediaries — never appearing in the same sentence themselves — can still end up close in the refined space.

raw D(van, crucifix) — large, no direct co-occurrence
shortest path — van → helsing → must → crucifix
refined D(van, crucifix) — small, transitively connected
Clustering Algorithms

Four lenses on the same distance matrix

K-Means

centroid-based · k=5, n_init=10

Initialise k centroids, then alternate assignment and update until stable. Run on both the raw and Dijkstra-refined matrices as a fast baseline for direct comparison.

Hierarchical

agglomerative · dendrogram

Builds nested clusters bottom-up from the distance matrix, exposing the hierarchy between tight character clusters and broader thematic groupings.

Spectral

graph-theoretic · eigendecomposition

Uses the eigenstructure of the similarity graph to find clusters that aren't necessarily convex — well-suited to the irregular shapes the Dijkstra refinement produces.

DBSCAN

density-based · noise-aware

Groups dense neighbourhoods of words and explicitly flags outliers as noise rather than forcing every word into the nearest cluster.
